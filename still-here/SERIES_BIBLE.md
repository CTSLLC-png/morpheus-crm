# Still Here — Series Bible

The single source of truth for the *Still Here* animated series on YouTube.
Every episode, automated or hand-made, must conform to this document.

- **Channel:** Still Here — <https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCJiOGAr7cqMj2M6hZeamQRQ>
- **Creator:** Tyrone Pettway
- **Production tool:** AgentOpus Story Mode (`3d-animation` style)

---

## 1. Premise

*Still Here* is a short-form animated series for **children who have a parent
in prison** — and for the parents trying to stay present from the inside, or to
come home and show up.

The title is the thesis. The parent is still here. The child is still here. The
relationship did not end; it changed shape.

The series does not explain the criminal justice system, argue policy, or
assign blame. It sits with one child, in one small moment, and gives that
moment dignity.

## 2. Audience

| Lane | Who it's for | Voice | Share of slate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Child-facing** | Kids roughly 6–12 with an incarcerated parent | Warm, plain, second-person; speaks *to* the child | ~70% |
| **B — Parent-facing** | Fathers inside, or recently home, trying to reconnect | Direct, unsentimental, no lectures | ~30% |

Lane A is the heart of the channel. Lane B is what *Back in the Game* opened.
Never blend the two inside a single episode — pick a lane per episode.

## 3. Tone

**The register is Pixar, not public-service announcement.** Emotionally honest,
visually warm, and it earns its comfort rather than declaring it.

Do:
- Stay in one concrete moment. A drawing. A phone call. An empty chair at a table.
- Let the sadness be real before it is soothed.
- End on agency — something the child can *do*, or simply permission to feel.

Don't:
- Moralize, or explain the lesson the story already told.
- Use the word "inmate." Use *your dad*, *your mom*, *he's away*, *she's inside*.
- Depict cells, bars, cuffs, uniforms, or guards. Visiting rooms are shown only
  as warm, ordinary rooms with tables and chairs.
- Promise a release date, a homecoming, or that everything will be fine.

## 4. Recurring cast

Characters are anchored two ways: a written canon block (below, injected into
every render) and a reference image in the AgentOpus asset library. The canon
block is what keeps a character on-model when no anchor image exists yet.

### Maya — lead, Lane A
> A Black girl, about eight years old. Round cheeks, wide expressive dark-brown
> eyes, dark brown coils gathered into two puff-ball ponytails tied with
> mismatched yellow elastics. Wears a mustard-yellow long-sleeve tee, denim
> overalls with one strap loose, and scuffed white sneakers. Always has a
> dog-eared sketchbook and a stub of pencil. Small for her age; stands with her
> weight on one hip. Reads as thoughtful rather than sad.

Maya's device is **drawing**. She keeps her dad close by putting him on paper.
Established in *Maya Draws the Memories That Keep Her Dad Close*.

### Maya's dad — Lane A / Lane B bridge
> A Black man in his early thirties. Broad shoulders, close-cropped hair, a
> short beard, deep smile lines. Warm brown eyes that match Maya's. Wears a
> plain heather-grey crewneck sweatshirt. Gentle, deliberate, a little tired.
> Big hands that he uses carefully.

Never shown in custody. He appears in **memory, imagination, drawings, and
phone calls** — golden-lit, slightly softened, as Maya pictures him.

### Grandma Ruth — caregiver
> A Black woman in her early sixties. Silver-grey hair in a low bun, reading
> glasses on a beaded chain, a floral apron over a cardigan. Steady, dry
> humored, endlessly patient. The adult who is present.

The trusted-adult function. Maya brings her feelings to Ruth.

> **Casting rule:** at most three named characters per episode. Anchor images
> cap at 8 assets per render, and every extra character costs consistency.

## 5. Recurring locations

### Wilson's Bakery & Restaurant — the anchor location
Library asset `3c98b196fca1a9e1`. The real diner from the creator's childhood:
corner storefront, bright yellow illuminated **BAKERY & RESTAURANT** signage on
both faces, glass-enclosed sidewalk seating strung with warm white lights,
golden light on a wet street, brick above with fire escapes.

Wilson's is the emotional home base — where Maya and her dad went, and where
Maya and Grandma Ruth still go. **Feature it in roughly every third episode**
so the channel builds a recognizable place.

### Secondary sets
- **Maya's bedroom** — small, drawings taped over most of one wall, warm lamp.
- **The kitchen table** — where the phone sits, where the empty chair is.
- **The school hallway** — where other kids' questions happen.

## 6. Episode formula

Ninety seconds, five beats. This structure produced the existing episodes and
the automation depends on it.

1. **The ordinary moment** (0–15s) — Maya doing something small and specific.
2. **The ache** (15–35s) — what's missing surfaces. Name the feeling plainly.
3. **The turn** (35–55s) — a memory, an object, or Grandma Ruth shifts the frame.
4. **The act** (55–80s) — Maya *does* something. Draws, calls, saves a seat, tells.
5. **Still here** (80–90s) — land the title. Never as a slogan; as a fact.

Lane B swaps beats 1–2 for a direct address ("You missed it. That's real.") and
beats 4–5 for one concrete action the father can take this week.

## 7. Language rules

- Second person for Lane A. "You" is the child watching.
- Sentences short. One idea each. Read-aloud rhythm.
- No jargon: no *incarcerated*, *facility*, *correctional*, *sentence*, *custody*.
- Feelings get named: *missing*, *mad*, *embarrassed*, *worried*, *tired of explaining*.
- Never "don't be sad." Always "it makes sense that you're sad."

## 8. Safeguarding

This series speaks to vulnerable children. These are hard rules, not preferences.

- **Always route to a trusted adult.** Every Lane A episode names someone Maya
  tells — Grandma Ruth, a teacher, a counselor. The child is never alone with it.
- **No promises.** Not about release, homecoming, visits, or feelings passing.
- **No blame** — not toward the parent inside, the caregiver, or the child.
- **No crime detail.** What the parent did is never the subject and never named.
- **No fear imagery.** See §3. Nothing that would frighten a child about where
  their parent is.
- **No shame framing.** Having a parent in prison is never treated as the
  child's secret to keep or fault to carry.
- **YouTube "Made for Kids":** Lane A episodes are children's content. Set the
  audience flag accordingly at upload and keep comments closed.

## 9. Intellectual property

The series uses the **`3d-animation`** style — modern 3D feature animation with
warm cinematic lighting and expressive stylized characters. That look is the
house style.

All characters are **original creations of this series**. No Disney, Pixar, or
other studio-owned character, name, likeness, score, or dialogue may appear in
any episode, thumbnail, or title. This protects the channel from copyright
strikes and demonetization — which for a channel serving these families would
be an avoidable loss.

## 10. Titling and metadata

- **Title:** `Still Here: <specific moment>` — concrete over abstract.
  Good: *Still Here: The Empty Chair at Thanksgiving*.
  Weak: *Still Here: Dealing With Feelings*.
- **Never** put a child's real situation, a real prison, or a real name in metadata.
- Keep titles under 70 characters so they don't truncate.
- Lane B titles drop the *Still Here:* prefix and stand alone, as *Back in the
  Game* did.
